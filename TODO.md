# The Hundred: to do

Ideas from the owner. Nothing here is built until the owner says go.

## Built (kept here as a record)

- Siege from one to four gates, with great doors the foes march through in the
  skycam and the legion ringed at the centre, tough callings outermost.
- Cohort assembly: move heroes between cohorts by tap or drag. Gear now belongs
  to a calling, so it follows heroes into any cohort.
- Zone of control: entering a square next to an enemy ends the move, and
  walking away from an enemy gives it a free blow. Thieves and assassins slip
  away; fliers ignore it.
- Deployment by hand before the first turn.
- Vhorax as the day 31 foe, with two endings (kneel, or defy and win).
- Late days field tougher veterans instead of ever-larger hordes.

## Still open

- On Attack orders a ringed legion breaks formation at once. A "hold the ring"
  order, or a default of Hold on siege days, would suit sieges better.
- Balance has only been checked by the headless sim, which plays on standing
  orders. It wins the final day about half the time. Needs hands-on play.
- The foes' march through the doors only shows in the skycam, not on the
  tactical board.

## The Six: a party mode (after everything above)

A second mode in the same game, chosen on the title screen beside The Hundred.
Same arena, same Vhorax, same rules engine, its own save slot.

- **Hand control.** Six heroes, each acting by hand in initiative order, as in
  the gold-box games. No cohorts or standing orders. An optional "quick fight"
  button stands in for auto-battle.
- **Scale.** Smaller grid and a daily billing budgeted for six, so day 1 is
  still a few kobolds.
- **Party creation.** Pick six from the ten callings, name them, roll and
  reroll their stats.
- **Death.** Raisable for a price at the temple, and by the party's own healer
  once they reach a high enough level.
- **Spells.** Chosen each day from a spellbook and memorised, as in the
  gold-box games, instead of being automatic by level.
- **Figures on the board.** The tactical board shows the drawn figures instead
  of lettered tokens.
- **Paper doll.** A character sheet with about eight slots: weapon, off hand,
  head, body, cloak, boots and two rings. Equipping an item changes the figure
  on the doll, on the board and in the skycam.
- **Items and loot.** Each hero owns individual gear. The shop sells single
  items and foes drop loot, including magic items.
- The Hundred keeps its simpler cohort gear tiers.

### Side quests for The Six

Between arena days the party can leave the arena. These are billed as side
quests: a way to build up before the next arena battle.

- **Places.** A dungeon, a tower, a narrow slum street, and more in that vein.
- **Sense of place.** Each should feel like we are there, as far as the game
  can manage: its own map shape, scenery, colours and skycam treatment (tight
  corridors and doors in the dungeon, stacked floors in the tower, a long thin
  street with alleys and rooftops in the slum).
- **Enemies.** A good variety, suited to each place, not just the arena roster.
- **Reward.** Experience, gold and loot that carry back into the arena.

Open questions to settle before building:

- How the party leaves, given Vhorax holds them captive (his leave, a bribed
  gaoler, a secret way out).
- Whether a quest is one fight or a chain of rooms with rest in between.
- Whether quests cost time, so the next arena day arrives regardless.
- Whether heroes killed on a quest can still be raised.
